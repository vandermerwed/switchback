title: Workbook mode
description: Make a few pages to think through a problem on paper.
---

Ask for it directly ("let's work this through on paper"), or let the agent offer it when the
work is foggy, creative, decision-heavy, or has to be learned. A factual question or a mechanical
task is just answered; there is no workbook for those, and the agent should not slow you down for
one.

## What you say

Describe the problem the way you would to a colleague: "I can't decide whether to kill the sync
feature," "help me name this project," or "I need to actually remember this for the interview
next week." You do not need to name a style — the agent chooses one from the shape of the job —
but you can ask for one directly with `/switchback workbook`.

## What you get

One message with everything before anything is printed: the style and why, one line per page
telling you what it asks, which roles have no matching pen (printed as a circled letter instead),
and the folder the files will live in. Nothing is built until you say yes. If your desk has no
saved profile yet, the agent assumes a basic kit — A4, a black pen, a highlighter, a phone camera
— states that assumption, and asks one batched question about what else is on your desk.

Once you confirm, you get a PDF (or an HTML page with print steps, if no PDF renderer is
available), in a `switchback/<date>-<slug>/` folder. Most workbooks open with a cover that teaches
the colour language for that sitting, hold a handful of content pages built for your problem, and
close with a decision page, a blank question queue for your Ask pen, and a checklist of exactly
what to photograph afterwards — a Ritual is the exception: a single page, with no cover and no
closing pages.

## What you do with the paper

Print it, put your phone in another room, and work through it in the ink the cover describes.
Keep to the timebox on the cover and stop mid-sentence when it runs out — an unfinished sentence
is a real stopping point, not a failure. Cross-outs stay on the page; nothing gets tidied before
the photos are taken.

When you are done, photograph every page flat with its header visible and send them all back.
That hands you to [read mode](/docs/modes/read/).

## The four styles

Which style you get depends on the job:

| The job | Style |
| --- | --- |
| Decide, diagnose, or plan | **Sitting** — one session, opening to closing |
| Remember something over weeks | **Series** — three rounds, spaced out |
| Create, name, or invent | **Incubation** — a generating half, a real break, a judging half |
| Come back to the same question weekly | **Ritual** — one identical page, every time |

Each style's shape, cadence and starting pages are on the [styles page](/docs/styles/).

## Collections

A collection is a shelf of templates around one area: planning, product, learning, game
development and more. When your request fits one, the agent draws its pages from that shelf
first and names the collection in its confirmation message. You can also ask for one directly:
"use the game development collection". A collection never fences anything off, so any template
can still go in any workbook.

Some templates on a shelf are research-backed and some are practical formats that make no claim
(a game one-pager, a playtest sheet). Each page says which. Browse the shelves on the
[collections page](/docs/collections/), or run `switchback collections`.
