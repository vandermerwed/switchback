---
title: Read mode
description: How the agent reads your photographed pages back, without over-reading them.
---

Read mode is the return half of the loop. Most of the time you do not ask for it directly — you
send the photos of your marked-up pages, and the agent takes it from there — though you can also
ask for it directly with `/switchback read`.

## What you say

Just the photos: paths, or pasted images. Nothing else is required, though if more than one
workbook could match ("which sitting are these from?") the agent asks rather than guessing.

## What you get

One report, in a fixed order:

1. **The photo test** — what came through clearly, what did not, and where the agent was
   tempted to guess and chose not to.
2. **Your blue (Ask) questions, in number order** — answered before anything else.
3. **Page feedback** — anything you noted about the page itself, rather than about your problem,
   kept in its own table so it never gets mistaken for content.
4. **The content** — your decisions, your ideas, the reds still open, the crossed-out items kept
   visible, quoting your own words wherever they carry the weight.
5. **The next step** — another round, a hold, or, for a proof, the action queue built from your
   marks.

Two files get written for you: `W<n>.readback.md` holds the report, and `carry.md` tracks what
should follow you into the next sitting — open objections, answered questions, anything crossed
out but worth remembering.

## What the agent does with your marks

The cover's ink swatches (the boxes you coloured with your own pens) are read first, as the
ground truth for which colour means which role on that particular print. A stroke lighter than
the rest of the page is read as pencil — provisional, not yet trusted. Struck-out words stay
struck out; the agent does not try to recover what you meant to erase, and an illegible word is
quoted as illegible and asked about rather than guessed.

If one of your questions needs an outside source rather than recall, it may be handed to a
research step that runs in the background while the rest of your report comes back immediately.

## Common ways this goes wrong

Photos taken at an angle, in poor light, or with a colour that could be mistaken for another
(blue and purple, red and orange) can make a mark ambiguous. When that happens on something that
matters, the agent asks for one re-shoot of that page rather than guessing which role you meant.
