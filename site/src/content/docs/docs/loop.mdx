title: The loop
description: How the round trip between agent and paper works, and why the friction is deliberate.
---

```
 /switchback ──▶ printer ──▶ your pen ──▶ phone camera ──▶ /switchback
 the agent        you print    you think in     you photograph     the agent reads
 makes the pages               ink, away from   the pages          the marks back
                               the screen
```

Working with agents can turn much of the day into a stream of hand-offs: ask, wait, check, ask
again. There is little room left to sit with one hard problem for long enough to think it
through. Switchback puts a small amount of deliberate friction back only where the thinking
happens: the sitting, the pen, the walk away from the screen, and the photo you have to take to
get an answer.

That is the motivation for building it. It is not a measured claim that Switchback makes anyone
more productive, and the site does not make that claim either.

## Why paper, specifically

The agent could just print a document and ask you to read it, but a document you read is not the
same as a page you fill in by hand. Each printed page asks for your thinking; it never prefills
the answer, recommends an option, or states a conclusion. One page holds one thinking move, so
working through a workbook means making several small decisions in ink instead of one long scroll
of text.

The printer only prints black on white. Every meaningful mark on the page afterwards is your own
ink, in a small colour language described on the [ink page](/docs/ink/). That is deliberate too:
colour makes it obvious, at a glance, which parts of the page are the agent's structure and which
are your thinking.

## Why the read-back is ordered

When the photos come back, the agent does not summarise the whole page. It works in a fixed
order: first the questions you wrote in your Ask pen, then the rest of what you marked, then a
note about what the photos captured well and what they did not. That order exists so that the
thing you actually asked for is never buried under everything else the agent noticed.

Marks about the page itself — "these columns are too small," "this was not a good question" —
are kept separate from marks about your problem. The former is feedback on the workbook; the
latter is the content the next step is built from. Conflating the two would mean product feedback
quietly reshaping your decision, which the agent is built not to do.

## What carries forward

Objections you raised and never resolved, decisions you locked in with a circle, and anything you
crossed out but kept visible are all carried into `carry.md` in the workbook's folder. A Series or
Ritual reads that file back at the start of its next round, so an unresolved concern does not
quietly vanish between sittings — it either gets answered, or it comes back the next time you sit
down with the pages.
