---
title: Styles
description: Sitting, Series, Incubation, Ritual and Proof — shape, cadence, and when each one is used.
---

A workbook style is the shape a sitting takes over time. The CLI enforces each style's opening,
closing and page budget directly: a spec that breaks a style's rules is refused with a message
naming what is wrong and why.

## Sitting — decide, diagnose, plan

One session, up to ten pages including its cover and closing. It opens with a cover and closes
with a decision page, a blank question queue and a return checklist. In between, the pages are
chosen for the job: emptying out everything you think first, then narrowing — scoring options
against criteria, surfacing the assumptions a plan rests on, or imagining it is six months later
and it failed.

Use it for a single decision, a diagnosis, or a plan you need to leave the sitting having
committed to.

## Series — remember something over weeks

Three rounds of up to six pages each. Round two falls about a day after round one, round three
about three days after round two, and, if the deadline allows it, a fourth round about a week
after that. Every round opens with a recall page — write what you remember before you check
anything — because testing yourself before reviewing is a large part of what makes the spacing
work.

Use it for material you need to actually retain: an interview, an exam, anything you cannot
afford to have faded by the time it matters.

## Incubation — create, name, invent

Up to ten pages split into two halves by a single required break: a generating half where nothing
judges, ranks or ships, then a real break — a walk, chores, sleep on it — then a judging half that
works on what surfaced. The CLI refuses a judging page before the break and a generating page
after it, and it refuses a timer before the break, since time pressure while generating tends to
narrow rather than help.

Use it when you are naming, inventing, or need more raw material before you can judge any of it
fairly.

## Ritual — the same question, weekly

One page, no cover, no closing pages: a check-in or a scoresheet, identical every time you print
it. The point of the repetition is that change becomes visible by flipping back through past
rounds, which an unstructured page never gives you.

Use it for anything you want to track weekly without redesigning the page each time.

## Proof — review a document

Not a workbook in the same sense — this is [proof mode](/docs/modes/proof/): numbered paragraphs
with wide margins, built to be marked up rather than composed page by page. Up to ten pages per
part; a longer document is split into several parts, each built and handed back together.

Every style's shape rests on a claim the [evidence page](/docs/evidence/) grades honestly, from
A (robust) down to D (an established practice with no direct study behind it). Weak evidence is
labelled on the page it supports, never hidden.
