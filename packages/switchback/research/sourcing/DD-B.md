### Replication
Veinott, Klein & Wiggins (2010), "Evaluating the Effectiveness of the PreMortem Technique on Plan Confidence" (ISCRAM 2010 conference paper) is the seminal experimental test (178 participants, H1N1 university-response plan, conditions: standard critique / pros-and-cons / cons-only / premortem). The Gallop et al. (2016) and Peabody dissertation studies above are treated as the verified stand-ins for a "large-n" test of the same technique, and are broadly consistent with Veinott et al.'s unverified finding.

### Backfire evidence
- Sanna, Schwarz & Stocker (2002), "When debiasing backfires: Accessible content and accessibility experiences in debiasing hindsight," *J. Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 28(3):497–502. DOI `10.1037/0278-7393.28.3.497` — **verified** (title/author/year match). **Gap-fill (2026-09-25): closed — this exact quote was independently fetched verbatim in batch DD-A (also assigned to this agent), under `consider-the-opposite` and `hindsight-record`, and is reused here rather than re-fetched.** Verbatim: "Listing many counterfactual thoughts was experienced as difficult and consistently increased the hindsight bias, presumably because the experienced difficulty suggested that there were not many ways in which the event might have turned out otherwise. Directly on point for the brief's question: does struggling to generate many failure reasons make failure feel *less* likely? Answer: yes — struggling to generate many reasons increases (not decreases) confidence that the outcome was inevitable, via a felt-difficulty-as-information mechanism.

The mechanistic root of the Sanna et al. backfire: subjective ease of recall, not just recalled content, is used as a judgment cue. Not independently re-fetched for a verbatim quote this session (well-established, frequently-cited theory paper).

## decision-diagnosis/mere-urgency

**Construct:** the mere urgency effect · **Used by:** eisenhower (LB)

### Replication
**This is the strong point of this claim's evidence base.** Mills, Cubitt & Starmer (2026) is exactly the large independent replication the sourcing brief asked to search for: n=2,374, multiple controlled variants, a dedicated "fresh eyes" design, and a meta-analytic synthesis across all of them — and it **confirms** the effect, while noting attenuation once experimenter-demand and other confounds are controlled for. Result: **replicated (attenuated)**.

However, **the specific outcome-salience experiment (the paper's Experiment 3) was not reached this session** — the fetched PDF/mirror cuts off at Experiment 2B (p.680 of 690), before Experiment 3's methods and results.
